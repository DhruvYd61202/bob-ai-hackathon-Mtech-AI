# Problem Statement: Deepfake Detection for Criminal Investigations

## The Specific Audience Affected
Law enforcement agencies, particularly cyber cell officers, digital forensic analysts, and investigators handling cases of digital extortion, fraud, and misinformation. Victims of sextortion and deepfake blackmail are also deeply affected by the lack of rapid resolution.

## Why Existing Solutions Don't Solve It
Current methods rely heavily on manual frame-by-frame analysis by stretched digital forensic laboratories. The analysis is slow, and officers lack structured frameworks to understand AI-generated compression artifacts, facial landmark anomalies, and audio-lip sync discrepancies. Most open-source deepfake detectors only output a single percentage score without providing court-admissible explainability, making them difficult to use legally.

## Quantified Pain
- Sextortion gangs using AI-generated blackmail target thousands of victims. 
- Overloaded Forensic Science Laboratories (FSLs) face massive backlogs, delaying deepfake and media testing by weeks or months, resulting in stalled investigations.
- Without a structured legal packaging tool, even when deepfakes are identified, translating the technical evidence into IT Act 2000 / BNS sections is an arduous manual process.

## Why This Problem Matters Now
The barrier to entry for generating hyper-realistic synthetic media has fallen dramatically. What used to take a Hollywood studio months can now be done in minutes using open-source GenAI tools. We are seeing a surge in cases like the viral Rashmika Mandanna deepfake (Nov 2023). Without a robust, load-bearing AI tool to combat malicious AI, the legal and investigative systems will quickly become overwhelmed.
